# After the Transfer — The Flow

**From transfer day to the 20-week anatomy scan, in order.**
For a day-5 blastocyst transfer · male (XY) result · singleton pregnancy.

> **Education, not medical advice.** Your clinic's plan outranks every date and number here. Dates are typical windows, not orders. In an emergency, call 911 or go to the ER.

**How to read this:** each phase has **what is happening**, **what to do**, **what to ask**, and **the emotional weather**. The dates in the example column use a transfer on **October 10, 2026** → **EDD June 28, 2027**.

---

## The whole map

## Phase 0 — Transfer day (day 0)

**What is happening.** A day-5 blastocyst is placed in the uterus. At this moment you are **2 weeks 5 days** pregnant by IVF dating — pregnancy is counted from the last menstrual period, not conception.

**What to do.**
- Follow your clinic's transfer-day instructions (full bladder, medication timing, rest).
- Continue progesterone and estrogen exactly as prescribed.
- Expect light spotting or cramping from the procedure. Report any bleeding anyway.
- Do not test at home yet; a trigger shot can cause a false positive.

**What to ask.** "When is my first beta? When do I stop or taper each medication? Am I on pelvic rest, and for how long?"

**Emotional weather.** Relief, disbelief, terror. All three can coexist. The transfer is a beginning, not a verdict.

---

## Phase 1 — The beta wait (days 9–11)

**What is happening.** Your clinic draws a quantitative hCG. Many use **9–11 days post-transfer**; some do a day-7/8 preliminary. A single number at this stage is a starting point, not a prognosis.

**What to do.**
- Eat and drink normally unless told otherwise.
- Write down the number, the date, and the time.
- Repeat in **48 hours**.
- Do not compare your number to someone else's on the internet. The trend is the unit.

**What to ask.** "When will I get the result? Who calls? When is the repeat? When do we stop betas and switch to ultrasound?"

**Emotional weather.** The 48-hour wait is its own kind of loud. Plan one distraction block per day, not a full schedule.

<details>
<summary><strong>What the numbers mean (and don't)</strong></summary>

- hCG roughly doubles every **48–72 hours** very early, then slows.
- Minimum expected rise in 48 hours: about **53%** at low levels, **49%** under 1,500, **40%** from 1,500–3,000, **33%** above 3,000 mIU/mL.
- Above roughly **6,000–10,000**, doubling time stops being meaningful. Ultrasound takes over.
- A single beta cannot tell you whether the pregnancy is inside the uterus. A strong number does not rule out an ectopic; a low first number does not doom anything.
- About **1% of viable pregnancies** rise more slowly than the 53% rule. A slower rise is a "we follow up" signal, not a verdict.

</details>

## Phase 2 — The trend (days 11–18)

**What is happening.** A second beta, then sometimes one or two more every 48–72 hours, until the level is high enough to see a gestational sac on ultrasound. The **discriminatory zone** is often cited as **3,500 mIU/mL** (some clinics use 1,500–2,000).

**What to do.**
- Keep every result in one place: date, time, number.
- Ask for the **percentage rise**, not just "it went up."
- If the rise is slower than expected, ask what the plan is — do not self-diagnose.
- Watch for ectopic symptoms: one-sided pain, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness, fainting → **ER now**.

**What to ask.** "What rise are you looking for? At what level do we scan? If the rise is slow, what is the next step?"

**Emotional weather.** Numbers can feel like a report card. They are not. They are one instrument among several.

---

## Phase 3 — The first look (days 23–29 · 6w0d–6w6d)

**What is happening.** A transvaginal ultrasound looks for, in order:

1. Gestational sac
2. Yolk sac
3. Fetal pole
4. Cardiac activity

All four rarely appear at once at exactly 6w0d.

**What to do.**
- Confirm the appointment and bladder instructions.
- Bring one written question and, if allowed, one support person.
- Plan a gentle rest of the day afterward.
- If the scan is **indeterminate**, book the repeat before you leave.

**What to ask.** "What are we looking for today? What would make you ask me back? If it is indeterminate, when is the repeat and what will you look for?"

**Emotional weather.** This is the highest-stakes appointment of early pregnancy. It is also the one most likely to be inconclusive — not because something is wrong, but because 6 weeks is early.

<details>
<summary><strong>If there is no heartbeat yet</strong></summary>

The thresholds are deliberately conservative — built so a healthy pregnancy is never accidentally declared failed.

- **Diagnostic of early loss:** CRL ≥7 mm with no cardiac activity; or mean sac diameter ≥25 mm with no embryo.
- **Suspicious, not diagnostic:** anything smaller and quiet → repeat scan in **7–14 days**.
- **Indeterminate findings are followed, never declared.**

A healthy embryo grows about **1 mm per day** in this window. Seven to fourteen days is enormous measurable progress.

</details>

<a id="waiting-plan"></a>

### The waiting plan (for the 7–14 days after an indeterminate scan)

1. **A 15-minute daily worry window** at a set time.
2. **One real distraction block per day** — a walk, a show, one errand.
3. **One human to text instead of Googling.** A nurse line counts.

Write the worst-case fear down **once**, read it **once**, done. A pre-decided plan beats "we'll figure it out if it's bad."

## Phase 4 — The handoff (days 37–51 · 8–10 weeks, range 8–12)

**What is happening.** Your RE closes out and your OB takes over. Records transfer, a last scan if scheduled, the medication taper plan, and your dating.

**What to do.**
- Get the **exact graduation week** and the **records handoff** confirmed.
- Ask whether the RE stays reachable.
- Book the first OB visit before you leave.
- Bring the [My Protocol](DASHBOARD.md#my-protocol) table.
- Confirm the OB's after-hours number.

**What to ask.** "What week do I graduate? What gets sent to the OB, and to whom? Who do I call after hours now? Is the RE still reachable?"

**Emotional weather.** Relief mixed with abandonment. The "dumped" feeling is common after IVF. It is the end of a very supported period, not a demotion.

---

## Phase 5 — Screening (day 51+ · from 10 weeks)

**What is happening.** NIPT/cfDNA draws one tube of blood and reads placental DNA fragments. It screens for trisomy 21, 18, 13, and optionally sex chromosomes and microdeletions.

**What to do.**
- Confirm you are at least **10 weeks**.
- Ask which panel and what it includes.
- Ask about the **fetal fraction** and the **no-call** policy.
- Keep the anatomy scan on the calendar — NIPT is not a substitute.

**What to ask.** "Which NIPT panel? What does it screen? What happens if the fetal fraction is low or the result is no-call?"

**Emotional weather.** Waiting for a "boy" result can be joyful and terrifying at once. A no-call is not bad news; it means "not enough sample from that draw."

<details>
<summary><strong>Boy-specific NIPT notes</strong></summary>

- A "male" result means Y-chromosome DNA was detected.
- With an adequate fetal fraction, the call is about **98–99% accurate**.
- Caveats: low fetal fraction, vanishing twin, prior transplant or transfusion, sex-chromosome anomalies.
- First-trimester ultrasound cannot reliably distinguish genitalia. A "12-week nub" guess is not a method.
- The **18–22-week anatomy scan** is the visual confirmation.

</details>

---

## Phase 6 — The neck scan (days 58–78 · 11w0d–13w6d)

**What is happening.** A specialized ultrasound measures the nuchal translucency (fluid behind the fetal neck). It requires a CRL of **45–84 mm** to be valid. It is often paired with a blood screen (PAPP-A and free beta-hCG) for a combined risk figure.

**What to do.**
- Confirm the window and whether blood screening is also planned.
- Allow 45 minutes.
- Ask what a "borderline" result would mean and what the next step is.
- Remember: it is a **screen**, not a diagnosis.

**What to ask.** "Are we doing NT alone, NT plus blood, NIPT, or both? What would a borderline result trigger?"

**Emotional weather.** Another scan, another wait. The same waiting plan applies.

---

## Phase 7 — The quiet stretch (weeks 14–17)

**What is happening.** Growth is steady; visits space out. Many people feel better physically and more anxious psychologically, because there is less to "do."

**What to do.**
- Add one gentle routine, not five.
- Sleep on either side; left-side optimization is a third-trimester thing.
- Book the anatomy scan if it is not on the calendar.
- Notice one good thing a week.
- If quiet days are heavy, book a brief check-in. You do not need a crisis to deserve support.

**What to ask.** "What is the next milestone, and what should I watch for between now and then?"

**Emotional weather.** The absence of appointments can feel like the absence of evidence. That is a design flaw in early pregnancy care, not a sign about your baby.

---

## Phase 8 — The anatomy scan (days 107–135 · 18–22 weeks)

**What is happening.** A 30–45-minute scan checks organs, growth, placenta position, and — for a boy — visibly identifies penis and scrotum. The testes will be described as inside the abdomen; that is normal at this stage. A small hydrocele is common and usually resolves.

**What to do.**
- Confirm the date and whether your support person can attend.
- Ask about bladder prep; wear a two-piece outfit.
- Bring water and your question list.
- Ask for the report in writing if you want to read it later.
- Plan a slow afternoon.

**What to ask.** "Is everything measuring in range? Is there anything you want to re-check? What is the plan for the next four weeks?"

**Emotional weather.** Often the first time the baby feels specific. It can also be the scan most likely to produce a "let's look again" — which is routine, not a verdict.

<details>
<summary><strong>Boy-specific anatomy notes</strong></summary>

- Visible penis and scrotum confirm what NIPT predicted.
- **Testes inside the abdomen** at 18–22 weeks are normal; descent happens in the third trimester, sometimes after birth.
- **Hydrocele** (a little fluid around the scrotum) is common and usually resolves before or shortly after birth.
- Occasionally a detail needs a second look. Thousands of anatomy scans get re-checked without drama.

</details>

## Phase 9 — After 22 weeks

Your OB is leading. The same two-tier safety rules still apply. The dashboard's week cards end at 22; your care does not.

---

## Your daily flow

### Morning (5 minutes)

1. **Check the one thing.** Open the dashboard; do the first action if there is one.
2. **Meds.** Take what is due; log it.
3. **Water and a small meal.** Protein at breakfast if you can.
4. **Notice one thing** that is not pregnancy — the light, the weather, a song.

### Evening (5 minutes)

1. **Meds.** PIO if prescribed; rotate sides; warm compress.
2. **Check-in.** 30 seconds: nausea, fatigue, anxiety, spotting, cramps.
3. **Close the loop.** If you logged a Tier-1 symptom, call the clinic today.
4. **Worry window.** 15 minutes, then close the note.
5. **Stop.** One thing is enough. You do not have to earn rest.

### Weekly reset (10 minutes)

- [ ] Count medication supply; order if under 7 days.
- [ ] Review the next appointment and its prep card.
- [ ] Empty the sharps container if needed.
- [ ] Check the calendar for the next scan window.
- [ ] Write one sentence about the week.
- [ ] Choose one thing to look forward to.

## Decision trees

### If you see spotting

```
Any spotting?
├── Soaking a pad in ≤1 hour, clots, or gray/white tissue
│   └── TIER 2 → Call 911 or go to the ER now
├── Spots or light flow + dizzy/faint
│   └── TIER 2 → Call 911 or go to the ER now
└── Spots or light flow, no dizziness
    └── TIER 1 → Call your clinic today (every time, even light or brown)
```

### If you have cramps

```
Cramping?
├── Severe, one-sided, shoulder-tip pain, or dizzy/faint
│   └── TIER 2 → Call 911 or go to the ER now
├── Moderate, both-sided, no shoulder tip, no dizziness
│   └── TIER 1 → Call your clinic today
└── Mild, both-sided, on-and-off
    └── Common (uterine stretching) → mention at next visit; call if it changes
```

### If you miss a dose

```
What did you miss?
├── PIO (any gap)
│   └── Call the clinic (even after hours).
│       They will say skip-and-resume or take it late. Never double.
├── Vaginal progesterone or estradiol, <24 h
│   └── Take it late today. Never double.
├── Vaginal progesterone or estradiol, >24 h
│   └── Call before restarting. Never cold turkey.
└── Aspirin or prenatal
    ├── Same day → take it late
    └── More than a day → skip and resume
```

### If your scan is indeterminate

```
No heartbeat yet?
├── CRL ≥7 mm OR mean sac ≥25 mm
│   └── Diagnostic criteria met → your clinic will discuss next steps
└── Smaller / cannot measure yet
    └── Suspicious, not diagnostic → repeat scan in 7–14 days
        ├── Set a 15-minute worry window
        ├── One distraction block per day
        └── One person to text instead of Googling
```

### If NIPT is no-call

```
No-call / low fetal fraction
├── Not bad news — not enough placental DNA in that draw
├── Ask about a redraw (most succeed)
├── Ask what the fetal fraction was
└── Keep the anatomy scan on the calendar
```

### If you feel abandoned at graduation

```
Graduation feelings
├── Name it: common after IVF
├── Confirm OB first visit + after-hours number
├── Ask if the RE stays reachable
├── Bring My Protocol
└── Plan one small ritual to mark the handoff
```

### If anxiety spirals at 2 a.m.

```
2 a.m. spiral
├── Open Safety (one tap)
├── Text your scan partner
├── Write the worst case once; read it once
├── Box breathing: 4-4-4-4, four rounds
├── Pick tomorrow's worry-window time
└── If you have thoughts of self-harm → 988 / OB / ER now
```

## The emotional weather map

| Phase | Common weather | What helps |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer → beta | Hope, dread, superstition | One distraction block a day; no home testing early |
| Beta wait | Obsessive number-checking | Record the pair; one call with the nurse |
| First scan | Terror, then relief or limbo | Written question; support person; waiting plan |
| Graduation | Relief + abandonment | Records checklist; one handoff ritual |
| NIPT wait | Hope + fear of "no-call" | Know the no-call plan before the draw |
| NT window | Scanxiety returns | Same waiting plan; 45-minute expectation |
| Quiet stretch | "Is it still there?" | One good thing a week; brief check-in if heavy |
| Anatomy scan | Excitement + vulnerability | Support person; slow afternoon; written report |

## What "normal" is not

- **Normal is not a guarantee.** The dashboard describes patterns, not promises.
- **No symptoms is not a bad sign.** Around 20–30% of pregnant people never get nausea.
- **A single number is not a verdict.** The trend, the scan, and the clinical picture are the picture.
- **A missed dose is not a failure.** It is a clinic call.
- **A bad scan day is not a diagnosis.** Indeterminate findings are followed, not declared.
- **You are not "too anxious."** About 1 in 5 pregnant women experience clinically significant anxiety. Help exists.

## Closing

The first trimester is mostly waiting, with a few intense appointments. The goal of this flow is not to make you calm by pretending the stakes are low. It is to make the next step obvious, the red flags impossible to miss, and the waiting a little less lonely.

> **This tracker is education, not medical advice.** Your clinic's instructions always outrank anything here. If something feels wrong, call your clinic. If it is an emergency, call 911 or go to the ER.
