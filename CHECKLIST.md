# After the Transfer — Checklist

**A printable, tappable companion to the [dashboard](DASHBOARD.md).**

> **Education, not medical advice.** Your clinic's instructions always outrank this list. Do not start, stop, or change a medication without your RE or OB. In an emergency, call 911 or go to the ER.

## The daily six (the whole list, honestly)

- [ ] **Medication** — take what is due, as prescribed. Log it.
- [ ] **Water** — 8 glasses; sips count.
- [ ] **Food** — one thing that stays down. Protein at breakfast if you can.
- [ ] **Movement** — a gentle walk or stretch, if you feel up to it. Rest counts too.
- [ ] **Check-in** — 30 seconds: nausea, fatigue, anxiety, spotting, cramps.
- [ ] **Stop** — one thing is enough. You do not have to earn rest.

> **A missed box is not a failed day.** This list is a rhythm, not a grade.

## Weekly checklist

### Week 4 — the waiting week

- [ ] First beta scheduled or done.
- [ ] Second beta on the calendar, 48 hours later.
- [ ] Clinic number and after-hours number saved in my phone.
- [ ] Medication supply counted; refill buffer ≥7 days.
- [ ] One low-effort comfort plan for the waiting days.
- [ ] No Googling after 10 p.m. (one rule, not a moral test).

### Week 5

- [ ] Betas trending; ask when the first scan will be.
- [ ] First ultrasound date on the calendar.
- [ ] Start the [worry window](DASHBOARD.md#checkin) if anxiety is loud.
- [ ] Water and small meals; protein at breakfast if tolerated.
- [ ] Tell one trusted person the scan date — or choose not to.

### Week 6 — first scan window

- [ ] First ultrasound (6w0d–6w6d typical).
- [ ] Ask: "What are we looking for today, and what would make you ask me back?"
- [ ] Bring one written question; you will not remember it on the table.
- [ ] Plan something gentle after the appointment — not a full day.
- [ ] If the scan is indeterminate: repeat scan booked in 7–14 days. See [waiting plan](DASHBOARD.md#waiting-plan).

### Week 7

- [ ] If a repeat scan was needed, it is booked.
- [ ] Refill check again; PIO compounding can take lead time.
- [ ] Ask about the graduation week and records handoff.
- [ ] One walk, one nap, one thing that is not about pregnancy.

### Week 8

- [ ] Graduation week confirmed? If not, ask this week.
- [ ] NIPT timing confirmed (from 10 weeks).
- [ ] NT scan timing confirmed (11w0d–13w6d).
- [ ] Pelvic rest instructions confirmed, if any.
- [ ] Taper plan: exact week and method, in writing.

### Week 9

- [ ] Graduation scan / handoff.
- [ ] Records sent to the OB? Confirm.
- [ ] First OB appointment booked.
- [ ] My Protocol table filled in and printed.
- [ ] Ask: "Who do I call after hours now — RE or OB?"

### Week 10

- [ ] NIPT blood draw (from 10 weeks).
- [ ] Ask which panel, and what happens if fetal fraction is low.
- [ ] Confirm the OB's after-hours line.
- [ ] Medication taper continuing exactly as prescribed.

### Week 11

- [ ] NT scan booked (11w0d–13w6d).
- [ ] Ask whether the clinic wants NT + blood screen, NIPT, or both.
- [ ] Bring the medication list to every visit.
- [ ] One thing you are looking forward to this week.

### Week 12

- [ ] NT scan if not already done (window closes 13w6d).
- [ ] End-of-first-trimester medication review with your OB.
- [ ] Ask about safe exercise, pelvic rest, and sex — get a clear answer.
- [ ] If relatives ask for a "12-week gender scan": sex is not readable on ultrasound this week. NIPT is the source; the anatomy scan confirms at 18–22 weeks.

### Week 13

- [ ] First trimester complete — mark it somehow.
- [ ] Write down what got you through: people, routines, small comforts.
- [ ] Confirm the anatomy scan date (18–22 weeks).
- [ ] Ask about the first-trimester screening results if you did combined screening.

### Weeks 14–17 — the quiet stretch

- [ ] Anatomy scan on the calendar.
- [ ] Energy returning? Add one gentle routine, not five.
- [ ] Sleep on either side — left-side optimization is a third-trimester thing.
- [ ] Notice and log one good thing a week.
- [ ] If quiet days are heavy, book a brief check-in; you do not need a crisis to deserve support.

### Weeks 18–22 — anatomy scan window

- [ ] Anatomy scan booked (18–22 weeks; often 20).
- [ ] Ask: full bladder or not? Can my support person come?
- [ ] Wear a two-piece outfit; bring water.
- [ ] Write down the one sentence you want to remember.
- [ ] If a detail needs a second look, that is routine — not a verdict.
- [ ] Boy confirmation: penis and scrotum visible; testes inside the abdomen are normal at this stage; a small hydrocele is common and usually resolves.

## Appointment prep checklists

### Beta hCG

- [ ] Know the draw time; eat and drink normally unless told otherwise.
- [ ] Ask when the result will be available and who will call.
- [ ] Write down the number and the date.
- [ ] Do not judge a single number — the pair is the unit.
- [ ] Ask: "When is the next draw, and when do we switch to ultrasound?"

### First ultrasound (6–7 weeks)

- [ ] Confirm date, time, and location.
- [ ] Ask about bladder preparation.
- [ ] Bring one written question.
- [ ] Bring a support person if allowed.
- [ ] Plan a gentle rest of the day.
- [ ] Ask: "What are we looking for? What would make you ask me back?"
- [ ] If indeterminate: "When is the repeat scan, and what will you look for?"

### Graduation to OB

- [ ] Exact graduation week.
- [ ] Records transfer confirmed — what is sent, and to whom.
- [ ] Medication taper plan in writing.
- [ ] First OB visit booked.
- [ ] After-hours number for the OB.
- [ ] My Protocol table printed.
- [ ] Ask: "Is the RE still reachable for questions?"

### NIPT

- [ ] Confirm you are at least 10 weeks.
- [ ] Ask which panel and what it screens.
- [ ] Ask about fetal fraction and the no-call policy.
- [ ] Know that a no-call is not bad news; a redraw usually clears it.
- [ ] Do not use NIPT as a substitute for the anatomy scan.

### NT scan

- [ ] Confirm the window (11w0d–13w6d) and CRL requirement (45–84 mm).
- [ ] Ask whether blood screening is also being done.
- [ ] Allow 45 minutes.
- [ ] Ask what a "borderline" result would mean and what the next step is.
- [ ] Remember: it is a screen, not a diagnosis.

### Anatomy scan (18–22 weeks)

- [ ] Confirm date and whether the support person can attend.
- [ ] Ask about bladder prep.
- [ ] Wear a two-piece outfit.
- [ ] Bring water and a snack.
- [ ] Bring your question list.
- [ ] Ask for the report in writing if you want to read it later.
- [ ] Plan a slow afternoon afterward.

## Red-flag quick card

### Call 911 or go to the ER now

- [ ] Heavy bleeding — soaking a pad in an hour or less
- [ ] Clots or gray/white tissue
- [ ] Severe pain, one-sided pain, or shoulder-tip pain
- [ ] Fainting or near-fainting
- [ ] Fever ≥100.4°F / 38°C with any other symptom
- [ ] Can't keep fluids down for 24 hours, or can't pee
- [ ] Chest pain, trouble breathing at rest, or coughing blood
- [ ] Thoughts of harming yourself or the pregnancy → 988 / ER

### Call your clinic today

- [ ] Any spotting or bleeding, even light or brown
- [ ] New or different cramping; moderate pain
- [ ] Foul-smelling discharge
- [ ] Fever lasting 24 hours
- [ ] Food won't stay down, but fluids will
- [ ] One calf suddenly warm, swollen, or painful
- [ ] Anxiety you can't work, eat, or sleep through
- [ ] You are not sure — uncertainty counts

## Food safety checklist

- [ ] Cook meat, poultry, and eggs thoroughly (eggs firm; meat 165°F / 74°C).
- [ ] No unpasteurized dairy or juice; no soft cheese from unpasteurized milk.
- [ ] Deli meat and hot dogs reheated to steaming before eating.
- [ ] No raw sprouts, raw dough, or runny eggs.
- [ ] No refrigerated pâté or smoked fish unless cooked into a dish.
- [ ] Low-mercury fish only: salmon, sardines, shrimp, tilapia, pollock, catfish, light tuna.
- [ ] Avoid shark, swordfish, king mackerel, tilefish, bigeye tuna, marlin, orange roughy.
- [ ] Albacore tuna ≤6 oz/week.
- [ ] Caffeine ≤200 mg/day (about one 12-oz coffee).
- [ ] No alcohol.
- [ ] No herbal supplements without clearing them with your OB.
- [ ] No high-dose vitamin A supplements or retinoid skincare.
- [ ] Wash produce; wash hands after soil, raw meat, or cat litter.
- [ ] Avoid cat litter and garden soil if someone else can do it; if not, gloves and wash.
- [ ] Avoid sharing saliva with toddlers (CMV) — no tasting their spoon.
- [ ] Prenatal vitamin with at least 400 mcg folic acid daily.

## Pelvic rest, exercise, and daily life

- [ ] I know whether I am on pelvic rest. (Ask; do not guess.)
- [ ] Pelvic rest = nothing in the vagina, and often no orgasm. It is not universally required after IVF. If you are on vaginal progesterone, do not stop it — ask your RE how to give it during pelvic rest.
- [ ] Exercise: 150 minutes/week moderate if uncomplicated; walking, swimming, prenatal yoga.
- [ ] Avoid contact sports, hot yoga, scuba, and high fall-risk activities.
- [ ] No hot tubs or saunas.
- [ ] Sleep on either side; flat on your back is fine now.
- [ ] Dental care is safe — tell the dentist you are pregnant.
- [ ] Hair dye: ask your OB; generally avoid in the first trimester if you can.
- [ ] Travel: carry medications in carry-on; keep a prescription copy.
- [ ] Vaccines: flu and COVID are recommended; live vaccines are not. Ask about Tdap timing.
- [ ] Sex: only if you are not on pelvic rest and your OB has cleared it.

## Anxiety toolkit checklist

- [ ] Name the feeling: "This is anxiety, not evidence."
- [ ] Box breathing: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4 — four rounds.
- [ ] 5-4-3-2-1 grounding: 5 see, 4 touch, 3 hear, 2 smell, 1 taste.
- [ ] Set a 15-minute worry window for today.
- [ ] Text one person instead of Googling.
- [ ] Move your body for 10 minutes.
- [ ] Eat something, even small.
- [ ] Limit symptom-checking to one scheduled time.
- [ ] Write the worst case once; read it once; close the note.
- [ ] If anxiety is ≥8, cancel one non-essential thing today.
- [ ] If it is interfering with work, eating, or sleep for more than a few days, tell your OB. That is a same-day conversation.
- [ ] If you have thoughts of self-harm: 988, OB, or ER — now.

## Questions to bring to your RE

- [ ] What week do I graduate, and what gets sent to the OB?
- [ ] What exact week do I stop progesterone, and is it a taper or a hard stop?
- [ ] What is the plan if I miss a PIO dose?
- [ ] Am I on pelvic rest? For how long, and what exactly does it include?
- [ ] What exercise is cleared for me?
- [ ] When is my first scan, and what if it is indeterminate?
- [ ] When do I do NIPT, and which panel?
- [ ] When is my NT scan?
- [ ] What is the after-hours number, and when should I use it?
- [ ] Is the RE still reachable after graduation?

## Questions to bring to your OB

- [ ] What is the plan for the next four weeks?
- [ ] How do we handle the medication taper?
- [ ] What are my labor and delivery options, and when do we decide?
- [ ] What screenings are next (anatomy, glucose, Tdap, group B strep)?
- [ ] What symptoms should send me to triage versus call the office?
- [ ] Who covers after hours?
- [ ] Can I have my records and scan reports?

## Partner / support checklist

- [ ] Know the clinic and after-hours numbers.
- [ ] Know the medication schedule and where the supplies live.
- [ ] Learn the injection routine (or be the warm-compress person).
- [ ] Know the two-tier red flags.
- [ ] Drive to appointments when possible.
- [ ] Ask "do you want company, distraction, or quiet?" — not "are you okay?"
- [ ] Take one logistical task off her plate each week.
- [ ] Do not say "at least you're pregnant" or "just relax."
- [ ] If she is spiraling, offer a walk, a snack, or a phone call — not a lecture.
- [ ] Protect one uninterrupted rest block per day.
- [ ] Know that a missed dose or a bad day is not a failure of either of you.

## If this happens — quick flows

### I missed a dose

- [ ] Do not take two.
- [ ] Do not stop a hormone cold turkey.
- [ ] If it was PIO: call the clinic, even after hours. They will say skip-and-resume or take it late.
- [ ] If it was vaginal progesterone or estradiol and it has been more than 24 hours: call before restarting.
- [ ] If it was aspirin or a prenatal: take it late if it is the same day; otherwise skip and resume.
- [ ] Write down what happened and mention it at the next visit.

### I saw spotting

- [ ] Note the amount: spots / light flow / soaking a pad / clots.
- [ ] Note whether you are dizzy or fainting.
- [ ] Call the clinic today — every time, even light or brown.
- [ ] If soaking a pad in an hour, clots, or dizziness: ER now.
- [ ] Do not insert anything into the vagina until your clinic says it is okay.

### My scan was indeterminate

- [ ] Confirm the repeat scan date (7–14 days).
- [ ] Ask what the follow-up will look for.
- [ ] Set a 15-minute daily worry window.
- [ ] Pick one distraction block per day.
- [ ] Pick one person to text instead of Googling.
- [ ] Write the worst-case fear once; read it once.

### My NIPT was no-call

- [ ] It is not bad news; it means the sample did not have enough placental DNA.
- [ ] Ask about a redraw.
- [ ] Ask whether the fetal fraction was low and what that means for you.
- [ ] Do not self-interpret; the clinic reads it in context.
- [ ] Keep the anatomy scan on the calendar.

### I am graduating and feel abandoned

- [ ] Name it: this is a common post-IVF feeling.
- [ ] Confirm the OB's first visit and after-hours number.
- [ ] Ask whether the RE stays reachable.
- [ ] Bring the My Protocol table.
- [ ] Plan one small ritual to mark the handoff.
